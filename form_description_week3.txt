This report presents a time series forecasting study of monthly vehicle sales. I simulated seven years of monthly data (84 months) with an upward trend, yearly seasonality and an irregular lockdown-style shock, and deliberately injected missing months and outliers to demonstrate realistic data preparation. The data was put on a fixed monthly index, missing months were interpolated, outliers were detected with a 4-standard-deviation STL residual rule and replaced, and a log transform was applied to stabilise the multiplicative seasonality. I decomposed the series into trend, seasonality and noise with STL, finding a trend strength of 0.50 and a seasonality strength of 0.31, and used the Augmented Dickey-Fuller test to show that one regular and one seasonal difference make the series stationary. I then compared a seasonal naive baseline, Holt-Winters and a SARIMA model chosen by a grid search on AIC, validating all of them on a 12-month hold-out year using MAE, RMSE and MAPE. SARIMA was selected with a hold-out MAPE of 3.01 percent. Residual diagnostics were checked with the Ljung-Box test, and the final model was refitted on all data to forecast 2026 with 95 percent prediction intervals, giving about 209,432 units. The report includes code snippets, visualisations of historical data and forecasts, an interpretation of the results, and a discussion of the effectiveness and limitations of the model. The complete code is available in the linked GitHub repository.
